## Practical-operation and scoring precedents

[PR1297](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1297) was finally rejected for importance: aligning a windowed plate to illuminated crosses behind an arbitrary screen had no stated practical purpose beyond visual registration. The proposed operation here is recognisable deployment of a full-size free-standing privacy screen before changing or personal care. The target board samples the area to shield; registration of marks is not itself the task outcome. Recognition and importance remain reviewer judgments, not established by the geometric score.

[PR1683](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1683) was finally rejected because the distinguishing shade-selection challenge was visual reasoning, featureless6cm blocks did not represent the functional vaccine carrier/solar charger, and checking only charger corners allowed a shadow to cross its middle. Here the robot physically manipulates a linked, full-height, approximately9.95kg screen with supported feet, passive hinges and complete robot-release clearance. The opaque frame/inlay/foot geometry participates in the actual barrier. All567 finite sight segments are checked across the21-sample window, rather than only target corners; the coverage remains a discrete189-point/three-view approximation and does not prove privacy for every continuous target point, observer location or time between samples. These differences address the cited practical-operation and score-coverage concerns without claiming that occlusion itself is novel or that physical fidelity has been calibrated.

The public final feedback for these two precedents was read directly from the saved GitHub issue-comment responses; no private reviewer discussion is reproduced. Their team relationship to this candidate is unverified.
